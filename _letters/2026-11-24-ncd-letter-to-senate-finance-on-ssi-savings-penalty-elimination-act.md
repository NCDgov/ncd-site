Dear Chairman Crapo and Ranking Member Wyden:

I write on behalf of the National Council on Disability (NCD), an independent bipartisan federal agency with the statutory responsibility to advise the President, Congress, and federal agencies on all policy matters affecting people with disabilities, to urge your consideration of the SSI Savings Penalty Elimination Act (S. 1234) before the end of the session. The legislation would raise the Supplemental Security Income (SSI) asset limits for individuals and couples for the first time since 1989, without which beneficiaries must remain extremely low-income or spend down savings to qualify. As Americans’ cost of living and general inflation has inched higher each year, these now nearly 40-year-old asset limits are an out of touch high water mark that are overdue revisit by Congress.

NCD arrives at its policy advisement after careful research and investigation of topics of concern to disabled Americans, and in 2023, NCD issued Toward Economic Security: The Impact of Income and Asset Limits on People with Disabilities, in which we examined how strict asset caps in federal safety-net programs, such as SSI and non-MAGI Medicaid, restrict savings, employment, and economic independence for people with disabilities. [^1] The history of the asset limits involved an interest in ensuring that only the neediest received the benefit and that they were still able to maintain a “cushion” of savings in case of an emergency. In most instances, an emergency savings cushion is premised on consideration of one’s monthly expenses. In view of current rental expenses, grocery prices, and other monthly living expenses, it is plain to see how a 37-year-old asset limit of $2,000 for an individual and $3,000 for a married couple does not provide for such an emergency cushion and has significantly worsened the poverty trap of the program. In our 2023 report, NCD recommended that Congress should eliminate or index asset limits for SSI to inflation rates to ensure that people with disabilities can benefit from earnings increases without losing important benefits. [^2] S. 1234 would raise the limits to $10,000 for individuals and $20,000 for couples and permanently index both to inflation, in line with NCD’s recommendations.

This summer, NCD announced it had begun to develop a blueprint of actions federal policymakers could take to encourage creation of a disability middle class in America – one that incentivizes upward economic mobility in part by eliminating policy trip falls like the SSI asset limits, which result in disabled Americans deliberately curtailing their employment opportunities and all but guarantee persistent poverty to its beneficiaries. Although the framework is still under development, reconsidering asset limits will most certainly be a significant piece of our advisement.

Legislation intending to address the asset limits has been introduced in Congress for many years and has long enjoyed a wide, bipartisan base of support from Members, the disability community, the faith-community, and the business community. The prudence of raising the limits has been well researched, and advisement to pursue such action has come from many corners over the years, including from NCD. We encourage the Committee to consider this important piece of legislation yet this session, as an opportunity to take a vital step in the foundational work of creating a pathway for the upward economic mobility of millions of Americans with disabilities in each of our communities. 

If you have interest in a briefing on our 2023 report on this topic or any of our recommendations relating to the jurisdiction of your committee, please contact our Director of Legislative Affairs, Anne Sommers McIntosh, at amcintosh@ncd.gov. 

Respectfully,
